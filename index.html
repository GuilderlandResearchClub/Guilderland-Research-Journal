Microplastic Concentrations in the Normans Kill Watershed Across Four Seasons

Maya R. Chen · Grade 12 · Mentor: Dr. L. Okafor · Published 14 Sep 2026 · DOI 10.55555/ghsrj.2026.0412
Abstract. Question: Do microplastic counts in local stream water vary by season? Method: We sampled six sites quarterly and filtered 2 L per sample. Findings: Concentrations peaked after spring runoff, 3.1 times the autumn baseline. Conclusion: Stormwater inputs, not wastewater, best explain the seasonal pattern.

Introduction

Microplastics are plastic fragments under 5 mm that enter streams through runoff, laundry effluent, and tire wear. Few studies report seasonal data for small upstate tributaries.

Methods

Water was drawn 10 cm below the surface and vacuum-filtered through 0.45 µm membranes. Particles were counted under 40× magnification. Concentration was computed as:

C = N⁄V    (particles L−1)

Results

Mean concentration was highest in spring (Figure 1) and lowest in autumn.

Discussion

The spring peak coincides with snowmelt and heavy rain, which points to surface runoff as the main pathway. Sites downstream of the treatment plant showed no higher counts.

References

Browne, M. A., et al. (2011). Accumulation of microplastic on shorelines worldwide. Environmental Science & Technology, 45(21), 9175–9179.

Submission guidelines


How GHSRJ reviews student research and what we look for.


How review works


  1. Submit your paper on the Submit tab. An editor checks that it is complete and fits the journal.
  2. Two reviewers read it without seeing your name and score it on the criteria below.
  3. The editors email you their decision: accept, revise or decline.
  4. If revisions are requested, resubmit with a short note on what you changed. Accepted papers are published online.


What reviewers look for


  • Methodology: the question is clear and the methods fit it.
  • Clarity: the writing is organized and easy to follow.
  • Argumentation: the conclusions follow from the evidence.
  • Originality: the paper adds something beyond a summary of existing work.


Structure


  • Abstract of 150 to 250 words covering the question, methods, key findings and conclusion.
  • Sections in this order: Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, References.
  • Submit the manuscript as plain text or Markdown, and attach a PDF if you have one.


Figures


  • Every figure needs a title and a short description, and must be mentioned in the text.
  • Use clear images with readable labels, and only include figures you made or have permission to use.


Citing sources


  • Cite every source you use, in the text and in the References section.
  • Use one citation style consistently, such as APA or MLA.
  • Quote sparingly and always give credit. Work that is not your own will be declined.
